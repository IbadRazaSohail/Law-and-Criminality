02 / PERSPECTIVES

ONE ISSUE.
MANY LENSES.

What is a perspective? A perspective is a viewpoint shaped by who you are, where you live and what you experience. Comparing perspectives helps avoid one-sided conclusions and explains why people may disagree.

A / PERSONAL

The household

View: Crime means lost money, unexpected expenses, time lost to reporting and fear that changes routines.

Evidence: Our anonymous survey and interviews. In our survey, 32 of 54 respondents reported a robbery victimisation, and 26 of 54 said they had been affected by a gang fight. These self-reported findings describe our sample, not all households. Team survey, n = 54 respondents

Strength: Shows the human cost. Limit: Individual experiences do not represent everyone.

B / LOCAL & NATIONAL

Institutions

View: Authorities frame crime as a security challenge addressed through technology and operations such as S4 and CPK.

Evidence: S4 reported 381 cameras, 496 arrests and 349 vehicles seized by July 2026. CPK reported 2,000+ cameras in August 2026. Street-crime incidents fell from 71,105 to 64,323, but several measures ran at once.

Strength: Shows investment and operational activity. Limit: Outputs do not prove prevention; registered cases undercount actual crime.

WHERE THE PERSPECTIVES CLASH

  • Authorities report falling registered crime while families may still feel unsafe.
  • Official totals count recorded cases; many experiences go unreported.
  • Technology may support arrests, but families need recovery and justice.

Our conclusion

Surveillance has expanded policing capacity, but its independent effect on preventing crime is not yet proven. The cost to families continues in the meantime.
